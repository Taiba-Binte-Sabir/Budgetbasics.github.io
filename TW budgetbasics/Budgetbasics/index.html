Budgeting Basics

The six building blocks of a budget

Before you can budget well, you need to recognise these six concepts in your own money life.

Income

Money coming in — allowance, scholarship, part-time job, or internship stipend.

Fixed Expenses

Costs that stay roughly the same each month — rent/hostel, bus pass, subscriptions.

Variable Expenses

Costs that change month to month — food, outings, shopping, data top-ups.

Requirements (Needs)

Essential spending you can't skip without real consequences — food, transport, books.

Wants

Optional spending that makes life nicer but can wait — games, eating out, fashion.

Savings

Money set aside on purpose, before it gets spent — for goals or emergencies.

LEARN FROM COMMON MISTAKES

Common Money Mistakes

Small money mistakes can become habits. Learn how to recognize them and make smarter choices.

STUDENT SCENARIO

Sara goes to the mall after class and buys a pair of shoes she did not plan to purchase. Later, she realizes she needed that money for her monthly expenses.

CORRECTIVE ACTION

Pause before buying and ask yourself: “Do I need this right now?” Give yourself time to think before making an unplanned purchase.

STUDENT SCENARIO

Ali buys a small snack and a drink almost every day. Each purchase seems inexpensive, but together they take a noticeable part of his weekly spending.

CORRECTIVE ACTION

Keep track of small purchases for a week. Seeing the total can help you identify where you could reduce unnecessary spending.

STUDENT SCENARIO

Hamza forgets an important payment deadline because he does not keep track of due dates. He has to deal with an avoidable late charge.

CORRECTIVE ACTION

Write down important due dates or use reminders on your phone so payments are planned ahead of time.

STUDENT SCENARIO

Ayesha signs up for a streaming service during exams but stops using it afterward. The monthly charge continues because she forgets about the subscription.

CORRECTIVE ACTION

Review your subscriptions regularly and cancel services you no longer use or need.

STUDENT SCENARIO

Zain spends most of his pocket money during the first week of the month. By the final week, he has very little left for planned needs.

CORRECTIVE ACTION

Decide your priorities before spending. Set a simple weekly limit and keep some money aside for upcoming needs.

Small changes make a difference.

Recognizing a money mistake is the first step toward building a better financial habit.
